\begin{abstract}
This paper revisits the asset-pricing evidence in
\citet{maio_santaclara_2017}, who study short-term interest-rate innovations and
stock-market anomalies. The contribution is a replication-first extension: the
published design is audited before any temporal, regime, or high-frequency
interpretation is allowed to enter the evidence set. The current draft reports a
data-limited non-reproduction status rather than empirical magnitudes. The
article and publisher supplement are verified through local hashes, the table
targets are registered, and the estimation and validation code is tested, but
source-compatible factor, portfolio, first-pass, cross-sectional, and extension
artifacts are not yet available. The paper therefore identifies the claims that
can be tested, the inputs that still block those tests, and the conditions under
which a later empirical version may classify evidence as confirmation, narrowing,
reinterpretation, generalisation, contradiction, or missing-input
non-reproduction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Short-term interest rates are natural candidates for state variables in
intertemporal asset-pricing models. A shock to the investment opportunity set can
change discount rates, hedging demands, and the relative performance of portfolios
whose cash-flow profiles or balance-sheet exposures differ. The empirical
challenge is that a residual from an autoregressive model for a short rate is not
automatically an economically narrow object. It may summarize monetary-policy
surprises, central-bank information, macroeconomic news, data revisions, and
sample-specific persistence. A replication that treats this residual as a priced
factor must therefore separate the mechanical construction of the innovation from
the economic interpretation attached to it.

Maio and Santa-Clara study this problem in the setting of stock-market anomalies. % artifact: artifacts/evidence/article_manifest.json
This paper starts from their published article and asks a narrower but more
auditable question: when the baseline design is rebuilt from frozen sources, does
the short-rate innovation remain a stable priced factor once evidence is separated
by replication status, sample vintage, monetary regime, and high-frequency shock
component? The paper is deliberately sequential. A table cannot be called
reproduced until it is matched to a source-compatible input and an auditable
generated statistic. An extension cannot revise the baseline verdict. A causal
interpretation cannot be imported into the AR residual.

The current version is not an empirical-results paper. It is a working paper at
the evidence-freeze boundary. The repository has a complete publication-document
evidence pack and a tested pipeline for factor construction, portfolio assembly,
time-series estimation, cross-sectional pricing, robustness diagnostics,
temporal extension, monetary-regime analysis, shock decomposition, out-of-sample
falsification, manuscript checks, and release checks. What it does not yet have
is the source-compatible empirical panel required to populate the tables. The
result is a data-limited non-reproduction draft: the design is executable and
reviewable, while the numerical claims remain blocked.

\section{Contribution}

The first contribution is a table-level replication protocol. Rather than ask
whether the paper is broadly reproducible, the repository freezes individual
targets and assigns each one a status label. The allowed labels are reproduced,
approximately reproduced, missing-input, contradicted, and not-attempted. This
distinction matters because inaccessible author portfolios, ambiguous public
archive versions, and unpublished construction details are not the same as
statistical disagreement.

The second contribution is an extension design that preserves the baseline
vintage. The post-publication analysis is not allowed to overwrite the original
sample with revised data and then call the combined file a replication. Extension
evidence is recorded as new evidence, with its own sample window, source vintage,
asset universe, and uncertainty method.

The third contribution is an interpretation boundary. The short-rate residual in
the baseline is a rate innovation. A high-frequency decomposition can later split
announcement surprises into narrower components, but that identification exercise
belongs to a separate section and cannot be retrofitted into the baseline
language.

\section{Model}

The baseline model is the two-factor beta representation used for the replication
target. For portfolio \(i\), excess return \(R_i^e\), market factor \(m\), and
short-rate innovation \(u_r\), the time-series regression is
\[
R_{i,t}^e = a_i + \beta_{i,m}m_t + \beta_{i,r}u_{r,t} + \varepsilon_{i,t}.
\]
The cross-sectional pricing equation is
\[
E[R_i^e] = \lambda_0 + \beta_{i,m}\lambda_m + \beta_{i,r}\lambda_r + \alpha_i.
\]
The empirical question is whether the estimated \(\beta_{i,r}\) exposures help
explain average excess returns after accounting for market exposure and the
registered comparator models.

The strict baseline sample is configured from 1972-01 through 2013-12. % artifact: configs/baseline.yaml
For any baseline-results paragraph, the sample is the configured monthly
baseline, the model is the market-plus-short-rate pricing system, the first-pass
estimator is ordinary least squares with an intercept, the second-pass estimators
are the registered cross-sectional procedures, the test assets are the registered
anomaly portfolios, uncertainty uses the configured Newey-West and Shanken-style
adjustments where applicable, and economic magnitude is not reported until

\section{Replication Design}

The replication design has three layers. The first layer freezes the bibliographic
target and the table-level acceptance criteria. The second layer records every
source needed to build the baseline panel, including whether the exact definition
has been verified and whether redistribution is permitted. The third layer runs
the estimators and assigns table-level labels only after generated artifacts can
be compared to the registered targets.

The factor reconstruction starts from a monthly short-rate series and estimates
an autoregressive innovation with an intercept. The portfolio reconstruction uses
the configured anomaly portfolio sets and preserves the distinction between
public French-library data, author-provided portfolios, and WRDS-style
reconstructions. The estimator reconstruction uses time-series regressions for
betas and cross-sectional pricing regressions for risk prices and pricing errors.
Numerical tolerances are registered before results are interpreted.

\section{Robustness And Weak-Factor Design}

The robustness layer is designed to stop a high fitted cross section from being
overread. The pipeline records beta dispersion, beta-matrix rank, singular
values, factor spanning, influence diagnostics, leave-one-family pricing systems,
and familywise adjustments. The baseline factor must be strong enough, and
variation in the estimated loadings must be informative enough, before a risk
price can carry economic interpretation.

For robustness paragraphs, the sample is the generated strict baseline, the model

the baseline pricing artifacts, the test assets are the same assets used for the
replication audit, uncertainty is handled through weak-factor and familywise

\section{Monetary Regimes}

The regime analysis is an extension, not a baseline replication device. Regime
boundaries are declared before any pricing result is inspected, and the design
includes boundary-shift checks, pooled interactions, split-sample eligibility
rules, and structural-break diagnostics. A regime finding may narrow or
reinterpret the baseline evidence, but it cannot be used to relabel a published
table target.

For regime paragraphs, the sample is the extension-compatible monthly panel, the
model is the registered regime-interaction system, the estimator is pooled regime
pricing and break diagnostics, the test assets are compatible anomaly portfolios,
uncertainty is based on the registered Wald, Holm-adjusted, and break-test
procedures, and economic magnitude is blocked until regime inputs exist. % empirical-paragraph

\section{Interpretation}

The intended interpretive categories are confirmation, narrowing,
reinterpretation, generalisation, contradiction, and data-limited
non-reproduction. The current evidence supports only the last category. That is a
substantive statement about the repository state: the publication documents and
software gates are in place, while the source-compatible generated panels are not.
The appropriate conclusion is therefore bounded and procedural, not empirical.

The advantage of this conservative draft is that later results will have a clear
place to land. If the baseline tables reproduce within tolerance, the extension
can ask whether the same pricing structure survives later data and different
monetary regimes. If the baseline can only be reconstructed approximately, the
paper can still contribute by showing which inputs drive the difference. If the
short-rate factor is weak, unstable, or absorbed by high-frequency components,
that evidence will narrow the interpretation rather than being hidden.

\section{Limitations}

The main limitation is input availability. Exact public archive versions, author
or WRDS portfolio files, source-compatible extension panels, high-frequency event
files, and generated result cells are not yet available in the committed
artifacts. These limitations are recorded in
\artifact{research/data_access_matrix.csv}. The release gate reports no critical
restricted-path finding, but it retains one major missing-input finding that
blocks empirical-results release.

There are also econometric limitations that will remain relevant after data are
registered. Cross-sectional pricing depends on informative beta dispersion.
Regime samples may be short. Structural-break searches require multiplicity
control. High-frequency event surprises require careful aggregation. Comparator
models must use identical asset and date intersections. These are not optional
robustness checks; they are conditions for interpreting the short-rate innovation
as economically meaningful.

\section{Data Access Statement}

Restricted article and supplement files remain local-only. The committed
repository contains hashes and metadata but not copyrighted source documents,
prompt files, licensed data extracts, local catalogs, or temporary artifacts. The
data-acquisition guide explains which inputs must be obtained manually or
verified before redistribution.

\section{Hypothesis Registry}

Confirmatory and extension hypotheses are listed in

forecast performance.

\section{Robustness Appendix}

The robustness appendix must be generated from
\artifact{reports/generated/robustness_report.md} and registered robustness
tables once empirical artifacts exist. Null results, weak-factor warnings, and
instability are reportable outcomes.

\section{Table-Level Replication Appendix}

The table-level replication appendix must be generated from the registered
replication audit artifact. No baseline verdict can include temporal, regime,
shock-decomposition, or out-of-sample extension results.

\section{Conclusion}

This draft is a paper at the point where evidence discipline matters most. It
does not claim that short-rate innovations price anomaly returns in the current
repository state. It specifies how that claim will be tested, how replication and
extension evidence will be separated, and how unavailable inputs will be reported.
Once source-compatible empirical artifacts are generated, the same manuscript
structure can carry numerical results without changing the rules used to judge
them.
