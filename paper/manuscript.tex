\begin{abstract}
This manuscript extends the framework of \citet{maio_santaclara_2017} only after
the replication and extension artifacts are frozen. The current repository state
is a reproducible scaffold: the article and supplement are hashed locally, but
baseline replication, temporal extension, regime stability, shock decomposition,
and out-of-sample falsification remain blocked by missing empirical inputs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\citet{maio_santaclara_2017} study short-term interest-rate innovations and stock
market anomaly returns. This paper treats their published article as the starting
point, then separates exact replication, documented reconstruction, and new
extension evidence. The present draft contains no empirical finding not generated
from repository artifacts.

\section{Original Framework}

The baseline asset-pricing representation follows an intertemporal factor model:
\[
E[R_i^e] = \lambda_0 + \beta_{i,m}\lambda_m + \beta_{i,r}\lambda_r + \alpha_i.
\]
Here \(R_i^e\) is an excess return, \(\beta_{i,m}\) is the market loading,
\(\beta_{i,r}\) is the loading on a short-rate innovation, and \(\alpha_i\) is a
pricing error. The short-rate residual is described as a rate innovation, not as
an identified monetary-policy object.
