\begin{abstract}
This paper extends the framework of \citet{maio_santaclara_2017} by separating
strict replication, documented reconstruction, and new regime-oriented evidence.
The current frozen repository artifacts support a data-limited non-reproduction
draft. Publication documents are hashed locally, table targets are registered,
and the estimation code is tested, but empirical magnitudes are withheld until
factor, portfolio, first-pass, cross-sectional, and extension panels are generated
from auditable inputs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\citet{maio_santaclara_2017} study whether short-term interest-rate innovations
help price stock-market anomaly returns. This paper keeps that article as the
baseline target and adds three distinctions that are useful for a transparent
extension. First, exact replication is reserved for published targets generated
from source-compatible inputs. Second, documented reconstruction is used when an
input is unavailable and a substitute must be named. Third, extension evidence is
reported separately from the replication verdict.

The central question is whether the short-rate innovation behaves as a stable
priced state variable across monetary regimes, or whether its pricing content is
narrowed by source definitions, factor strength, sample windows, and shock
composition. The current answer is deliberately bounded. The repository can state
what is prepared, blocked, and falsifiable; it cannot report generated empirical
magnitudes until the missing inputs recorded in \artifact{research/data_access_matrix.csv}
are resolved.

\section{Original Framework}

The baseline pricing equation is written before discussing any table result:
\[
E[R_i^e] = \lambda_0 + \beta_{i,m}\lambda_m + \beta_{i,r}\lambda_r + \alpha_i.
\]
Here \(R_i^e\) denotes an excess return, \(\beta_{i,m}\) is the market loading,
\(\beta_{i,r}\) is the loading on a short-rate innovation, \(\lambda_m\) and
\(\lambda_r\) are cross-sectional prices, and \(\alpha_i\) is the pricing error.
The short-rate residual is a rate innovation relative to the fitted time-series
model. It is not an identified monetary-policy object in the baseline design.

The strict baseline sample is configured from 1972-01 through 2013-12. % artifact: configs/baseline.yaml

OLS first-pass regressions; test assets are the registered anomaly portfolios;
uncertainty is Newey-West and Shanken-style corrected where applicable; economic
magnitude is unavailable until generated artifacts exist. % empirical-paragraph

\section{Replication Design}

The replication design follows a strict separation between the published baseline
and later extensions. Table-level targets are registered in
\artifact{research/table_target_manifest.csv}. Each target can be classified only
as reproduced, approximately reproduced, missing-input, contradicted, or
not-attempted. Missing source data are reported as missing inputs rather than as
evidence against the article.

Factor reconstruction uses the configured short-rate source, an autoregressive
innovation model with an intercept, contemporaneous residual timing, and monthly
return units. Portfolio reconstruction uses the configured value-weighted anomaly
sets, with unavailable author or WRDS files held out until they are legally
registered or reconstructed under the documented reconstruction label.

\section{Monetary Regimes}

Regime boundaries are declared before results and sourced from recorded
policy-action evidence. The design allows boundary sensitivity, interaction
tests, split-sample eligibility checks, structural-break scans, and stability
classification. A stability result can confirm, narrow, reinterpret, or fail to
generalise the baseline evidence; it cannot change the baseline replication label.

Empirical context: sample is the extension-compatible monthly panel when
available; model is the registered regime-interaction system; estimator is pooled
regime pricing and break diagnostics; test assets are compatible anomaly
portfolios; uncertainty is Wald, Holm-adjusted, and break-test based; economic
magnitude is blocked until regime inputs exist. % empirical-paragraph

\section{Interpretation}

The paper will frame generated evidence through confirmation, narrowing,
reinterpretation, generalisation, or data-limited non-reproduction. In the current
draft, the only supported classification is data-limited non-reproduction. That
classification means the repository has not yet produced the empirical artifacts
needed for a replication verdict; it is not a claim that the article is
contradicted.

\section{Limitations}

Unavailable inputs include exact public archive versions, author or WRDS
portfolio files, source-compatible extension panels, high-frequency event files,
and frozen generated result cells. These limitations are recorded in
\artifact{research/data_access_matrix.csv}. The adversarial release gate reports
no critical restricted-path finding, but it classifies missing factor and
extension-panel artifacts as a major unresolved issue that blocks empirical
results release.

\section{Reproducibility Statement}

All empirical claims must be generated from versioned code, typed configuration,
and machine-readable artifacts. The validation status is recorded in
\artifact{VALIDATION.md}. The release gate, SBOM, and source checksum manifest
are generated by \artifact{artifacts/release/release_gate.json},
\artifact{artifacts/release/sbom.json}, and
\artifact{artifacts/release/source_artifact_checksums.sha256}.

\section{Data Access Statement}

Restricted article and supplement files remain local-only. The committed
repository contains hashes and metadata but not copyrighted source documents,
prompt files, licensed data extracts, local catalogs, or temporary artifacts.
Public acquisition remains disabled until exact source definitions and licence
terms are recorded.

\section{Hypothesis Registry}

Confirmatory and extension hypotheses are listed in
\artifact{research/hypothesis_registry.csv}. Hypotheses cannot be rewritten after
observing generated errors or instability diagnostics.

\section{Robustness Appendix}

The robustness appendix must be generated from
\artifact{reports/generated/robustness_report.md} and registered robustness
tables once empirical artifacts exist. Null results, weak-factor warnings, and
instability are reportable outcomes.

\section{Table-Level Replication Appendix}

The table-level replication appendix must be generated from
\artifact{artifacts/audit/table_replication.csv}. No baseline verdict can include
temporal, regime, shock-decomposition, or out-of-sample extension results.

\section{Conclusion}

The current repository supports a professional extension-paper draft whose
empirical conclusions are intentionally blocked. Its contribution at this stage
is a reproducible structure for separating exact replication, documented
reconstruction, and new extension evidence. Numerical conclusions will be added
only when the required artifacts are generated, mapped, and accepted by the
manuscript and release gates.
